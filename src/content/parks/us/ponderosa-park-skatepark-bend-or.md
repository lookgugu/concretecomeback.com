Set beneath the ponderosa pines at 225 SE 15th Street (near SE Wilson Avenue) in southeast Bend, Ponderosa Park Skatepark is a 9,000-square-foot cast-in-place concrete facility operated by the Bend Park and Recreation District (BPRD). Redesigned and rebuilt by Spohn Ranch Skateparks in 2013 to replace an aging 1997 slab, the park sits inside a 21.5-acre neighborhood park equipped with off-street parking, public restrooms, shaded picnic areas, and paved walking trails, serving as a central hub for older skaters in Central Oregon's high desert.

What makes Ponderosa especially welcoming for returning older skaters is its split-elevation layout and BPRD's rule prohibiting bicycles and motorized vehicles on the concrete skatepark. Along the upper edge of the facility, Spohn Ranch created a dedicated above-grade "skate path" lined with mellow pump bumps, gentle radiuses, and wide spacing where beginners and rusty returners can roll and carve in a continuous loop without crossing the main street and transition lines below. Once warmed up, you can drop into the lower street plaza of ledges, flat rails, banks, and stairs or pump the transition half-pipe and quarter-pipe pocket. Keep a few caveats in mind: while Ponderosa Park grounds are open daily from 5:00am to 10:00pm, the skatepark has no overhead night floodlighting, so sessions are limited to dry daylight hours; after-school hours draw steady local crowds; and Bend's 3,600-foot elevation brings winter snow and freeze-thaw ice that can close the outdoor concrete until spring. When travelling across the Cascades to the Willamette Valley, pair a Central Oregon trip with the covered, stadium-lit concrete at [WJ Skatepark + Urban Plaza](/directory/parks/us/wj-skatepark-eugene-or) in Eugene or visit [Cal Skate Skateboards](/directory/shops/us/cal-skate-portland-or) and [Ed Benedict Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/ed-benedict-skatepark-portland-or) in Portland.
