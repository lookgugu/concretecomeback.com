Located at 217 Schoenbar Road near Schoenbar Middle School, rotary ballfields, and the Schoenbar Trailhead in central Ketchikan, the Ketchikan Skatepark is a 5,643-square-foot cast-in-place concrete municipal skatepark converted from older modular ramps to smooth poured concrete in 2013. Equipped with an adjacent paved parking lot and user-activated push-button floodlights mounted on the parking-lot pole, it gives older skaters on Revillagigedo Island practical evening session time even when Southeast Alaska's daylight hours shrink in autumn and spring.

The poured-concrete footprint divides transition and street terrain cleanly across two main zones so returning older skaters can progress at a comfortable pace. A street course along the perimeter offers bank ramps, quarter-pipes, ledges, a flat rail, and a stair set with handrail, while the main concrete bowl steps from a forgiving 3-foot shallow end with roll-in access down to an 8-foot deep pocket for faster carving. Keep several local caveats in mind: the push-button floodlights operate on a one-hour timer after dark and flash an orange warning light on the pole shortly before shutting off, so press the button again before starting a run if the warning blinks; Ketchikan's temperate rainforest climate brings frequent rain, and the smooth concrete transitions become slick quickly when damp or covered in wet needles; and the park is unsupervised and shared with scooters and bikes after school. Elsewhere along the Inside Passage and Southcentral Alaska, you can pair a trip here with [Turnaround Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/turnaround-skate-park-sitka-ak) in Sitka, [Pipeline Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/pipeline-skate-park-juneau-ak) in Juneau, or [Taku Lake Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/taku-lake-skatepark-anchorage-ak) in Anchorage.
