Located at 160 Water Street next to Patricia T. Russell Park in downtown Keene, the Keene Skate Park is a municipal concrete facility managed by the Keene Parks & Recreation Department and open daily from 8:00am to 8:00pm. Connected by a paved pedestrian walkway to the Russell Park parking lot on Carpenter Street, the park sits beside public restrooms, a drinking water fountain, and a quarter-mile paved walking loop, giving older skaters in New Hampshire's Monadnock Region a convenient everyday spot to roll.

What makes the facility especially welcoming for returning older skaters is its deliberate entry progression. In July 2025, the City of Keene installed an enlarged, level asphalt apron at the skatepark entrance specifically designed to give less experienced skaters a smooth flatground zone to practice pushing, balance, and basic board control before moving onto the main concrete pad's quarter-pipes, bank ramps, ledges, and flat rails. Note a few practical caveats before heading out: the facility is unsupervised and explicitly shared with scooters, inline skates, and BMX riders; there are no stadium lights for late-night sessions after 8:00pm; and parking, restrooms, and water are located next door inside Patricia T. Russell Park (restrooms operate seasonally from spring through autumn). Skaters crossing southern New Hampshire on Route 101 can pair a Monadnock session here with [David W. Deane Skateboard Park](/directory/parks/us/david-w-deane-skateboard-park-nashua-nh) in Nashua, [Dover Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/dover-skatepark-nh) in Dover, or [Chris Rice Skateboard Park](/directory/parks/us/chris-rice-skateboard-park-portsmouth-nh) in Portsmouth.
