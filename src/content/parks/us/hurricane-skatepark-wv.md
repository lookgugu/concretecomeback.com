Positioned just inside the main entrance of Hurricane City Park at 3511 WV-34 (minutes off I-64 Exit 34 halfway between Charleston and Huntington), Hurricane Skatepark is a $700,000 cast-in-place concrete facility designed and built by Grindline Skateparks that opened in August 2023. Equipped with overhead LED lighting for evening sessions alongside paved municipal parking, a playground, walking trails, a splash pad, and public restrooms near the baseball scorekeepers building, it gives older skaters across Putnam, Kanawha, and Cabell counties a smooth modern home base for both daytime and after-work roll-ins.

Posted City of Hurricane Skatepark Rules and Regulations state directly that the park "has been designed for beginner to intermediate skill levels." Rather than intimidating vertical walls, the poured-concrete footprint pairs a roll-in flow and pocket bowl with an open street course of low hubbas, flat rails, manual pads, A-frame banks, and wide flatground runouts where returning older skaters can dial in carving and basic grinds without high-consequence drops. Keep a few city regulations and caveats in mind: the facility is unsupervised ("use at your own risk") and riding is restricted to posted operating hours; protective helmets, knee pads, elbow pads, and wrist guards are strongly recommended; only skateboards, inline skates, roller skates, scooters, and bicycles are permitted (no motorized devices or toys); no glass containers, food, or drinks are allowed on the skating surface; and park restrooms are closed during the winter off-season from late autumn through April 1. Elsewhere in West Virginia, you can pair a road trip here with [Fayetteville Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/fayetteville-skatepark-wv) in the New River Gorge, [Fort Neal Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/fort-neal-skate-park-parkersburg-wv) in Parkersburg, or [Wheeling Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/wheeling-skatepark-wv) in the Northern Panhandle.
