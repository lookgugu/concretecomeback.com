Tucked along West Loomis Road beside Interstate 41 in Greenfield—roughly 20 minutes south of downtown Milwaukee—The Turf Skatepark is one of the most storied transition complexes in the Midwest. Originally opened in 1987 as the Surfin' Turf before closing in the mid-1990s and having its concrete pools buried under gravel, the site was resurrected through a multi-decade advocacy push by Save The Turf and the City of Greenfield. Reopened as a free municipal park on June 7, 2025, the facility was rebuilt by Grindline Skateparks using the original 1980s blueprints to restore all five historic concrete bowls while adding a sixth modern flow bowl and a spacious street plaza.

For older skaters returning to transition or visiting to ride a piece of 1980s skateboarding history, the expansive concrete footprint provides clear progression and physical separation between zones. Rather than dropping straight into the deep Clover, Keyhole, or over-vertical Capsule bowls, you can warm up on the wide outer street plaza of ledges, rails, and banks, or ease into transition in the 4-foot-deep Lip Slide Gully—built with a gradual 30-foot entry ramp—and the 5-foot-deep Footie Bowl with its rounded edges and comma-shaped roll-in. Keep several City of Greenfield rules and practical caveats in mind: the outdoor park has no night floodlights and operates strictly from dawn to dusk; parking is in a gravel lot equipped with portable restrooms; BMX bikes are restricted to plastic pegs only (no metal pegs) and scooters are prohibited from grinding on concrete edges or coping; and summer evenings draw heavy crowds of local and visiting riders. For sessions further west across Wisconsin, pair a Milwaukee-area stop here with [Irwin A. and Robert D. Goodman Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/goodman-skatepark-madison-wi) in Madison or the skateboard-only concrete at [Sun Prairie Skateboard Park](/directory/parks/us/sun-prairie-skateboard-park-wi) in Sheehan Park.
