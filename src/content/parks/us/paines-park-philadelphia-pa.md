Paine's is a plaza, not a bowl park: quarterpipes, banks, ledges, curbs, manual pads, flat and handrails, a stair set and a wallride, spread across a riverfront site that doubles as a public park and connects straight onto the Schuylkill River Trail. If your comeback is about relearning ollies and rolling over ledges rather than dropping in, this is the Philadelphia park to start at — FDR, the city's famous DIY park, is a very different proposition.

It was conceived in 2002 by Franklin's Paine Skatepark Fund, the skater-run non-profit that later helped win a skate plaza at the Municipal Services Building, and it opened in 2013. Limited parking at the Waterworks Drive lot off Kelly Drive and paid parking at the Art Museum garage; SEPTA buses 43 and 38 stop nearby.
