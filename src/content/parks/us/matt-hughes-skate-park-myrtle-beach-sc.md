Located near the corner of 33rd Avenue North and Robert M. Grissom Parkway, Matt Hughes Skate Park is a free municipal concrete facility operated by the City of Myrtle Beach Parks, Recreation and Sports Tourism Department and dedicated in memory of local skateboarder Matthew Reed Hughes. Built and expanded in phases by 5th Pocket Skateparks with grant support from The Skatepark Project, it serves as the primary public concrete skatepark along South Carolina's Grand Strand between Wilmington and Charleston, opening at dawn for calm morning sessions before coastal heat and beach traffic build.

The two-phase concrete layout gives older skaters clear separation between low-impact street warmup obstacles and transition carving. You can start on the wide street plaza—which features a speed bump, slappy curbs, manual pads, low grind ledges, a flat rail, a funbox, kickers, banks, a handrail, and mellow quarter-pipe and mini-ramp transitions—before stepping into the dedicated second-phase concrete bowl that replaced the park's original metal ramp. Keep several municipal caveats in mind: while the park is equipped with overhead night lighting, the City of Myrtle Beach notes that the evening light shutoff time varies from day to day (and when the lighting system undergoes periodic maintenance, hours temporarily revert to sunrise through sunset), and the unsupervised all-wheel facility has limited shade during humid summer afternoons. Skaters traveling down Highway 17 along the Lowcountry coast can pair a Myrtle Beach session with [SK8 Charleston](/directory/parks/us/sk8-charleston-sc) or head inland to [Owens Field Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/owens-field-skatepark-columbia-sc) in Columbia.
