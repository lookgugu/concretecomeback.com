The Charles River Conservancy planned, designed and built Lynch on a former contaminated site at North Point Park, and the state's Department of Conservation and Recreation now runs it. The location under the bridge ramps is the practical draw: weather that empties other parks leaves large parts of this one skateable, and the lights extended to 10pm mean a session after work in winter is realistic.

The terrain covers most of what you might want to relearn — a bowl, a mini ramp, quarterpipes, banks, ledges, curbs, manual pads, flat and handrails, stairs and a wallride — and the Conservancy points to regular skate groups that welcome beginners on any wheels. Bring nothing that needs parking: street spaces are scarce and metered with a two-hour limit, and it is a short walk from the Museum of Science. Free walls for graffiti artists are part of the park's programme, so expect the paint to change.
