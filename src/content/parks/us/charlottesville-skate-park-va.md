Built into a wooded hillside spanning a 30-foot elevation change at the corner of the Route 250 Bypass and McIntire Road, the Charlottesville Skate Park (often called McIntire Skatepark) is a 30,000-square-foot poured-concrete facility designed with Stantec's Kanten Russell and Action Sports Design and operated by Charlottesville Parks & Recreation. Open daily from 8:00am to 10:00pm with overhead night lighting, the two-acre site gives older skaters in central Virginia calm morning sessions under mature deciduous trees as well as extended evening runs after work.

Because the layout is terraced across multiple levels, returning skaters can work on fundamentals in the upper and lower street plazas—starting on a gentle entry roll-in, beginner flatground section, double metal-edged ledges, a low box ledge, 3-foot concrete quarter-pipes, a small volcano, and flat rails—before rolling down into the separate bowl section, which pairs a flowing transition bowl with deeper pool-style pockets. Plan your arrival around the park's access layout: there is no curbside parking directly on the Route 250 Bypass, so park either at the McIntire Park East lot by the Charlottesville Rescue Squad (828 McIntire Road) or the West softball fields lot (1360 Rugby Avenue) and follow the paved path and pedestrian bridge to the concrete. The park is a wheel-friendly facility shared with bikes, scooters, and inline skates, and City of Charlottesville rules strongly recommend helmets, knee pads, elbow pads, and wrist guards. Skaters travelling north toward the Washington DC metro can pair a central Virginia trip with [Powhatan Springs Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/powhatan-springs-skate-park-arlington-va) in Arlington or [Wakefield Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/wakefield-skatepark-annandale-va) in Annandale.
