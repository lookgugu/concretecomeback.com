Tucked into the west side of 145-acre Bob Noble Park behind the ball fields and directly beside the Greenway Trail entrance, the Paducah Skatepark (refreshed in 2022 with fresh concrete and new obstacles as the Bob Moore Kick Flip Skate Park) is a free municipal concrete facility operated by Paducah Parks and Recreation. For older skaters in western Kentucky, the park offers a well-maintained concrete slab with overhead night lighting managed by Park Rangers, a restroom building immediately next to the fence, spectator benches, and ample off-street parking inside Paducah's largest community park.

The layout blends open flatground approaches with banks, quarter-pipes, grind ledges, manual pads, and rails so returning skaters can warm up their legs on straightforward lines without dropping into deep vertical bowls. Plan around strict municipal ordinances enforced on site: under the Paducah Code of Ordinances, every skatepark user (whether on a skateboard, roller skates, or bicycle) is legally required to wear a protective helmet—and no spectator is permitted within 10 feet of the skating surface without a helmet—while elbow, knee, and wrist pads are strongly encouraged. In addition, Paducah Parks and Recreation enforces a zero-tolerance graffiti policy that closes the entire skatepark until any graffiti is removed, prohibits food, drink, glass, pets, and motorized devices inside the fenced area, and bars riding during or after rain, snow, or ice. If you are traveling east across Kentucky on the Western Kentucky Parkway and I-165, pair your trip with [Bowling Green Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/bowling-green-skate-park-ky) at Roland Bland Park or head up I-65 to [David Armstrong Extreme Park](/directory/parks/us/david-armstrong-extreme-park-louisville-ky) in Louisville.
