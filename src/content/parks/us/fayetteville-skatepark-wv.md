Located inside Charlie McCoy Town Park at 330 Park Drive on the rim of the New River Gorge National Park and Preserve, the Fayetteville Skatepark & Paved Pump Track gives southern West Virginia riders two distinct wheel-sports venues side by side: a custom cast-in-place concrete skatepark built by Grindline Skateparks and an adjacent 10,000-square-foot paved asphalt pump track with a wallride berm extension. For older skaters rebuilding leg stamina and carving rhythm, the separated 10,000-square-foot pump track offers a continuous, low-impact circuit of rollers and banked berms where you can build speed without taking flatground impact, while the concrete skatepark's outer street section provides mellow banks, ledges, flat rails, and a Jersey barrier away from the deep transition pockets.

Once warmed up, transition riders can step into Grindline's three-bowl complex, which progresses from a flow bowl with a volcano and window to an egg bowl finished with tile and brick coping and a steeper kidney bowl with pool coping. Keep Town of Fayetteville park rules and caveats in mind: the complex is an unlit, self-policing facility open dawn to dusk only ("skate at your own risk"), helmets, knee and elbow pads, and wrist guards are highly recommended, waxing the concrete is prohibited, spectators and pets must remain outside the active concrete skating area, and motorized vehicles and glass containers are barred. Skaters exploring West Virginia can also pair a New River Gorge trip with the lit Grindline concrete at [Fort Neal Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/fort-neal-skate-park-parkersburg-wv) in Parkersburg or [Wheeling Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/wheeling-skatepark-wv) in Elm Grove.
