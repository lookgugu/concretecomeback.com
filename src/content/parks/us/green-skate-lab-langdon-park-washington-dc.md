Tucked into the wooded park grounds behind the Langdon Park Community Center and Chuck Brown Memorial in Northeast Washington's Ward 5 (reachable from the Rhode Island Ave–Brentwood Red Line Metro station), the Green Skate Lab—also known locally as the Green Bowl or Langdon Park Skatepark—is an above-ground poured-concrete skate bowl operating under the DC Department of Parks and Recreation (DPR). Originally launched as America's first "green" community-built skatepark project using reclaimed materials and rammed-earth/recycled-tire foundations, the standalone transition bowl gives older skaters in the District a quieter, tree-shaded carving refuge away from the heavier downtown street-plaza crowds.

The multi-level concrete bowl is naturally zoned for progressive transition riding: returning skaters can start at the forgiving 3-to-4-foot shallow end with accessible roll-in spots and pump over the 3.5-foot center spine and roller before testing the 9-foot deep end finished with real pool coping. Keep several practical caveats in mind before visiting: the bowl is entirely transition-focused with no flatground street plaza, it has no dedicated night floodlighting so sessions are limited to dawn-to-dusk daylight hours, fallen leaves and tree debris after rainstorms sometimes require a quick broom sweep, and entering the adjacent Langdon Park Community Center for indoor restrooms requires a valid government-issued photo ID during open center hours. For flatground and street-plaza lines in the District, pair a bowl session here with [Shaw Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/shaw-skatepark-washington-dc) in Northwest D.C. or the bike- and scooter-free [Skate Park at RFK Campus](/directory/parks/us/skate-park-at-rfk-campus-washington-dc) on East Capitol Street.
