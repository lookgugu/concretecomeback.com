Set directly behind the Activity Center at Bohrer Park at Summit Hall Farm (510 South Frederick Avenue) in Gaithersburg, the Gaithersburg Skate Park is a 12,300-square-foot staffed outdoor municipal skatepark operated by the City of Gaithersburg Department of Parks, Recreation and Culture. Unlike unmonitored concrete slabs, the facility features wood-frame ramps topped with forgiving Skatelite composite panels—offering a lower-impact surface when stepping off—and provides structured School of Skate beginner clinics as well as customizable one-hour private lessons arranged through city skatepark staff (`skatepark@gaithersburgmd.gov`) from April through October.

For older skaters getting their feet back under them in Montgomery County, the ramp layout spans a wide range of heights so you can progress gradually. You can start on the open flatground, low banks, and smaller quarter-pipes or work on pumping inside the mini half-pipe before moving to the pyramid wedge, funboxes, A-frame ramp and rail, stairs, wallride, and 7.5-foot escalator. Keep several City of Gaithersburg rules and seasonal caveats in mind before heading over: the skatepark operates seasonally from late March through October 31 and closes completely for winter; hours are limited to daylight sessions ending at sunset (with Mondays and Tuesdays closed in summer and Monday through Thursday closed in fall); the park closes during rain, snow, or excessive heat; and every rider must have a completed liability waiver on file and wear a helmet at all times (with elbow and knee pads strongly recommended). When the seasonal ramps are closed or you want a lit evening session until 10:00pm, head east across Montgomery County to the skateboard-and-inline-only concrete at [Olney Manor Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/olney-manor-skate-park-md) in Olney, or drive south into Virginia to [Powhatan Springs Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/powhatan-springs-skate-park-arlington-va) in Arlington.
