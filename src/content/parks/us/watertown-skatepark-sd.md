Located at 314 21st Street SW just west of the Koch Complex and operated by the City of Watertown Parks, Recreation, and Forestry Department, Watertown Skatepark is a 12,000-square-foot all-concrete skatepark and pump-track hybrid designed and built by Evergreen Skateparks. Explicitly engineered to welcome all ages and ability levels, the poured-concrete course combines a 4-foot mini-ramp that flows into a bank with a tabletop and quarter-pipes, seven 4-to-5-foot quarter-pipes, a 2.5-foot launch tabletop, three approachable 18-to-30-inch pyramids (two topped with grind boxes), banks, and rails. For older skaters getting back on a board, the low pyramids, 4-foot mini-ramp, and pump-track transitions allow continuous low-impact carving without requiring high-consequence drops.

The facility is free to use and open daily from dawn to dusk, giving early risers reliable quiet morning sessions before afternoon traffic arrives. City of Watertown skatepark rules designate the slab for skateboarding, inline skating, and BMX biking while prohibiting scooters, which significantly reduces unpredictable cross-traffic. Keep the city's posted operational rules in mind before rolling: helmets are required for all skaters (with wrist supports, knee pads, and elbow pads recommended), the park is unlit (`Daily dawn to dusk`), glass containers, alcohol, tobacco, and graffiti are prohibited, and the facility closes whenever surfaces are wet or icy, during park maintenance, or during scheduled activities. Skaters driving the I-29 corridor in eastern South Dakota can pair a session in Watertown with [Dwiggins-Medary Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/dwiggins-medary-skatepark-brookings-sd) in Brookings or [Barb Iverson Skate Plaza](/directory/parks/us/barb-iverson-skate-plaza-sioux-falls-sd) in Sioux Falls.
