Tucked inside the 95-acre Dana L. Thompson Memorial Park ("Rec. Park") at 340 Rec Park Road off Route 7A in Manchester Center, the Manchester Skatepark is a 16,000-plus-square-foot cast-in-place concrete facility designed and built by Grindline Skateparks for the Town of Manchester. Surrounded by mountain views, ample off-street parking, seasonal restrooms, a municipal swimming pool, and walking paths, it serves as a central gathering spot for older skaters across southwestern Vermont and the Battenkill Valley.

Because the park was constructed across two distinct stages (a 6,000-square-foot Phase I completed in 2019 followed by a 10,000-plus-square-foot Phase II and III expansion finished in 2022), the footprint naturally separates lower-consequence terrain from heavier transition lines. Returning older skaters can warm up on the Phase I street plaza's mellow banks, ledges, flat rails, and low quarter-pipes before dropping into the expanded flow course and deeper pocketed bowls. Keep several town rules and operational caveats in mind: although the wider Rec. Park remains open until 10:00pm, the skatepark itself is unlit and restricted to sunrise-to-sunset hours; the facility is unsupervised ("skate at your own risk") and helmets, knee pads, elbow pads, and wrist guards are strongly recommended; all electric or motorized vehicles are strictly prohibited on the concrete; and pets, glass containers, and smoking are banned inside the skatepark perimeter. For a full Route 7 corridor trip, pair a session here with [Bennington Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/bennington-skatepark-vt) 25 miles south, or head north to [Andy A-Dog Williams Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/andy-a-dog-williams-burlington-vt) in Burlington and [Talent Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/talent-skatepark-south-burlington-vt) in South Burlington.
