Developed through a partnership between the City of Newark, the Thomas J. Evans Foundation, and local action-sports advocates about 35 miles east of Columbus, Everett Park Skatepark is a roughly 16,000-square-foot custom poured-concrete facility designed and built by Spohn Ranch Skateparks. Equipped with overhead sports lighting that stays on until 10:00pm EST alongside shaded spectator seating and off-street park parking, the park gives older skaters in Licking County and east-central Ohio both calm dawn sessions and reliable evening windows after work.

Spohn Ranch engineered the layout around clear, progressive ability separation across multiple zones: returning riders can build leg stamina without ollies on the integrated concrete pump track, practice flatground, low manual pads, ledges, and flat rails in the street section, or drop into the beginner bowl with a mellow transfer spine before progressing to the intermediate bowl or the advanced pool-coped bowl and full-pipe. Keep a few operational caveats in mind: Everett Park is an unsupervised all-wheels facility open to skateboards, BMX bikes, scooters, inline and quad skates, and wheelchairs, so fair-weather afternoons after school dismissal can bring busy multi-wheel traffic, and the automated sports lights shut off promptly at 10:00pm EST. Skaters in the wider Columbus and central Ohio region can pair a session here with Spohn Ranch's lit [Dublin Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/dublin-skate-park-oh) on the northwest side of Columbus.
