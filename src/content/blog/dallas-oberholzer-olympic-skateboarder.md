For many older skaters, the quiet worry isn't falling—it is wondering what happens when the list of tricks you can still learn starts shrinking. Coming back to a board at forty and helping organise group sessions, I think a skating life measured only by whether your airs are higher this summer than last summer eventually boxes everyone in. South African skateboarder Dallas Oberholzer, now fifty-one, offers a much sturdier picture of what decades on a board can look like when your own runs are tied to the people learning beside you.

Born in Durban in June 1975, Oberholzer grew up skating in the 1980s when the sport was treated as an outsider curiosity in South Africa. International audiences mostly met him through his late-forties Olympic runs: as [Andy Bull reported in *The Guardian* at the Tokyo Games in August 2021](https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2021/aug/05/dallas-oberholzer-the-46-year-old-olympic-skateboarder-that-fended-off-a-jaguar), Oberholzer competed in men's park at age forty-six—the second-oldest man in the Tokyo field behind Denmark's Rune Glifberg—and treated the heat like a shared skate session, cheering teenagers a third his age. Three years later, [Voice of America covered his return at the Paris 2024 Olympics](https://www.voanews.com/a/older-guys-macdonald-and-oberholzer-set-a-gold-standard-for-skateboarding-fun-at-the-paris-olympics/7733577.html) at age forty-nine, where he skated in the men's park preliminaries alongside fifty-one-year-old Andy Macdonald and posted his personal-best Olympic score of 33.83.

## The work that outlasts contest heats

Dropping into an Olympic bowl twice in your late forties is remarkable, yet the most durable part of Oberholzer's skating life happens far from broadcast cameras. As *The Guardian* noted in its 2021 profile, years before the Olympics he walked into a rural Zulu village in KwaZulu-Natal with his skateboard and began teaching local kids to ride—work that grew into his Indigo youth skateboarding movement.

That grassroots coaching continued after his second Games. In early 2025, the [City of Cape Town's Recreation and Parks Department teamed up with the Indigo Youth Movement and local group Outlangish](https://www.capetown.gov.za/media-and-news/skateboarding-workshop-pops-and-grinds/) to run free skateboarding workshops and provide boards for more than fifty young people from Langa, starting with a pilot session at the Langa Indoor Sport Complex pump track and following up the next weekend at the City Bowl Park. Well after his Olympic heats, Oberholzer's movement was still focused on helping beginners step onto a board for the first time.

For anyone [looking for other older skaters](/blog/finding-other-adult-skaters/) in their forties or fifties, that is the part of Oberholzer's career worth borrowing. You do not need an Olympic credential or a non-profit programme to outgrow the trap of treating every session as a private test of whether you are still as quick as you used to be. A session holds up over the long haul when the experienced person on the deck takes a few minutes to welcome a nervous newcomer, share space on the flat bank, and make sure the person next to them has a reason to come back next week.
