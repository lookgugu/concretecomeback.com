Once you can pump a mini ramp up to the coping and kickturn comfortably in both directions, the next milestone almost every returning skater eyes is getting both trucks up onto an edge: an **axle stall** on a mellow mini-ramp coping or a low **50-50 grind** on a waxed parking-lot curb. Locking both trucks onto steel or concrete feels intimidating after forty because a half-committed approach leaves one truck hanging up while your momentum keeps moving. Breaking the movement down into stationary lock-in drills, heel-side weight placement, and low-consequence exits lets you learn axle stalls and 50-50s methodically without gambling your wrists or hips.

## Gear and prerequisites before you touch the coping

Do not attempt coping stalls or curb grinds until two foundational habits are automatic:

1. **Pumping and kickturning near the top of a transition:** You should already feel comfortable building speed from the flat bottom and kickturning just below the coping, as covered in our [guide to pumping a mini ramp without dropping in](/blog/how-to-pump-mini-ramp-bowl-without-dropping-in/) and our [beginner trick ladder for older skaters](/blog/beginner-trick-ladder-for-adult-skaters/).
2. **Full transition protective gear and practiced knee-pad slides:** Wear a dual-certified skateboard helmet (CPSC 1203 / ASTM F1492), EN 14120 rigid-splint wrist guards, hardshell knee pads, and [impact shorts](/blog/impact-shorts-hip-protectors-for-adult-skaters/) (see our [protective gear guide](/blog/safety-gear-guide/) and [falling safely guide](/blog/how-to-fall-skateboarding-adults/)).

## Step 1: Feel the cross-lock on a stationary curb or coping

Before rolling at a ramp wall or curb, place your board directly on top of the coping or curb edge while standing still so both trucks sit in a 50-50 position, then step onto the bolts with a wall, fence, or friend nearby to steady your hand.

### Cross-locking your trucks so the board cannot slip sideways

On round metal coping, a truck hanger can slide left or right until a wheel bumps against the pipe. Pushing your **back wheel lightly against the inside of the coping** while turning your **front wheel slightly over the top edge of the coping** creates a two-point **cross-lock** that stops the board from sliding out in either direction. Spend two minutes stepping up into that locked position and feeling where your heels and toes sit over the bolts.

## Step 2: Learning the backside axle stall on a mellow mini ramp

Start on the lowest, mellowest quarter pipe or mini ramp at your park (the round-coping wooden transitions at [FLUX in Hamden](/directory/parks/us/flux-indoor-skatepark-hamden-ct/) suit the stall, and the slappy curb and low grind ledges at [Riverside Skatepark in Cedar Rapids](/directory/parks/us/riverside-skatepark-cedar-rapids-ia/) suit the curb version).

### 1. Approach at a slight angle with speed calibrated to the coping

Pump up the transition with just enough speed for your back truck to reach the top of the pipe. If you charge the wall with excess speed, your body will overshoot onto the flat deck behind the coping; if you under-pump, your back wheels will stop short of the pipe.

### 2. Lift the front wheels, lock the back truck, and shift onto your heels

As your back wheels crest the metal coping, lift the nose slightly so the front wheels clear the pipe, set both hangers down across the coping, and shift your center of mass onto your **heels** so your weight rests directly over the pipe. Standing flat-footed on top of the coping takes the downward pressure off the ramp wall and lets you pause for a full second.

### 3. Re-enter by looking back into the flat bottom, pressing the tail, and pivoting in

To come back into the ramp without hanging up your back wheels on the coping:

- Turn your head and lead shoulder back toward the flat bottom.
- Press down slightly on the tail pocket so the front wheels lift just enough to clear the metal pipe.
- Guide the front truck back into the transition curve and bend your knees as all four wheels touch the wall.

## Step 3: Turning the stall into a low-speed 50-50 grind

Once a stationary axle stall and re-entry feel repeatable, turning it into a moving 50-50 grind on coping or a waxed six-inch curb only changes your approach angle:

1. **Approach at a shallow 20- to 30-degree angle:** Instead of riding straight up the wall at 90 degrees, carve up the transition at a shallow angle so your momentum carries you parallel along the coping when your trucks lock in.
2. **Keep your weight centered between both bolts (50-50) with a slight heel bias on transition:** On a flat street curb, pop or ride onto the waxed edge with weight balanced evenly between your front and back trucks, then press the tail slightly at the end of the curb so both trucks drop off together rather than nose-diving.

Keep your axle-stall practice blocks short (10 to 15 minutes during the freshest part of your session after warming up), and end on a clean make while your legs still have full pop.
