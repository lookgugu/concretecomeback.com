Skateboarding ranks as moderate-to-vigorous physical activity on the exact metabolic scale exercise physiologists and physicians use to prescribe workouts. In the [2011 Compendium of Physical Activities (*Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21681120/), general skateboarding is coded at 5.0 METs and competitive skateboarding at 6.0 METs; in the updated [2024 Compendium of Physical Activities (*Journal of Sport and Health Science*)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38242596/) by Stephen Herrmann, Barbara Ainsworth, and colleagues, which compiled 1,114 activities for people aged 19 to 59 from published energy-expenditure measurements, general moderate-effort skateboarding stays at 5.0 METs and competitive skateboarding at 6.0 METs. Because public health guidelines define moderate exercise as 3.0 to 5.9 METs and vigorous activity as 6.0 METs and above, an active hour on your board counts toward your weekly cardiovascular and musculoskeletal training volume.

Skaters often ask me, half-apologetically, whether their skate sessions "really count" or whether they need to add a "legitimate" gym cardio session on top. Skating does not look like a structured workout because there is no console flashing your heart-rate zone and no instructor calling out intervals. Looking at the physiological numbers helps put that doubt to rest.

## Where skateboarding sits on the MET intensity scale

Exercise scientists compare activities using metabolic equivalents (METs), which express how much energy an activity requires relative to sitting quietly at rest (1.0 MET). Walking at a moderate pace on flat ground requires roughly 3.0 to 3.5 METs. Once an activity reaches 6.0 METs, six times your resting metabolic expenditure, clinical guidelines classify it at the threshold of vigorous physical activity.

Putting skateboarding at 5.0 to 6.0 METs places a steady skate session in the same metabolic neighborhood as brisk uphill walking or recreational doubles tennis. Casual flatground cruising sits toward the moderate end of that range, while continuous transition pumping, repeatedly driving through your quads and glutes to generate speed around a bowl or snake run without putting a foot down, pushes heart rate and oxygen demand into vigorous territory. We break down what that interval pattern does for aerobic capacity in our guide on [whether skateboarding builds your VO2 max](/blog/does-skateboarding-build-your-vo2-max/).

## Energy expenditure and the interval structure of a real session

Total energy expenditure during any session depends on body mass, terrain, and how much of the hour you spend moving versus resting on the deck talking with friends. Because 1 MET approximates 1 kilocalorie per kilogram of body weight per hour, an 80-kilogram skater riding continuously at a 5.0 to 6.0 MET pace expends roughly 400 to 480 kilocalories per hour of active riding.

A realistic park session is not sixty minutes of uninterrupted movement. You skate a 45- to 90-second run, catch your breath on the platform while two other riders take their lines, and drop back in. From a physiotherapy and conditioning standpoint, that work-to-rest ratio is not a flaw. Repeated high-effort bouts separated by two to three minutes of incomplete recovery train heart-rate recovery and lower-limb muscular endurance without the monotonous repetitive joint loading of an hour on pavement, a contrast we explore further in our comparison of [skateboarding vs. running after 40](/blog/skateboarding-vs-running-after-40/).

## Mechanical bone loading and reactive balance

Cardiovascular burn is only part of why clinicians care about weight-bearing exercise past 40. Bone tissue adapts to mechanical strain: when impact and muscle-pull forces load the hip and spine, osteocytes signal the skeleton to maintain bone mineral density. Purely non-weight-bearing endurance options such as swimming and stationary cycling are helpful for heart and lung conditioning, especially during early joint rehab, but they provide minimal osteogenic stimulus.

Skateboarding combines repeated unilateral pushing, eccentric quad and calf loading on transitions, and small, frequent ground-reaction forces. If you are managing low bone mass or osteopenia, talk with your clinician before counting skating as bone-loading exercise, and wear certified protective gear every session; see our clinical overview of [osteoporosis, bone density, and skateboarding](/blog/osteoporosis-bone-density-skateboarding/) along with our breakdown of [skateboarding as reactive balance training after 40](/blog/skateboarding-balance-training-over-40/).

## How to structure your sessions when fitness is a primary goal

Believing that skateboarding "doesn't really count" carries a real behavioral cost: skaters who view their board as guilty recreation often skip sessions when work gets busy, whereas treating it as legitimate physical training makes it easier to protect on your weekly calendar. At the same time, skating does not train every physiological system equally. It builds single-leg stamina, hip and ankle proprioception, and interval cardio, while doing very little for upper-body pulling strength or thoracic mobility.

If you want to lean into the conditioning side of skating, three practical habits raise the training value of an hour at the park:

### Favor continuous transition flow over single-trick flatground tries
Pumping a long concrete flow course or bowl keeps your large lower-body muscle groups under sustained tension for 45 to 60 seconds at a time. At sprawling concrete facilities like [Kona Skatepark](/directory/parks/us/kona-skatepark-jacksonville-fl/) in Jacksonville, with its historic concrete hills, snake run, and flow section, or the 40,000-square-foot [Arvada Skate Park](/directory/parks/us/arvada-skate-park-co/) in Colorado, where beginner-friendly mini-ramps and mellow transitions sit separate from the deep bowls, you can link turns continuously rather than stopping after every push.

### Use the board for short local errands
Replacing a 15-minute neighborhood drive with a brisk push on softer cruiser wheels turns dead errand time into steady 5.0-MET aerobic work. Our guide to [using a skateboard for daily transport after 40](/blog/skateboard-as-daily-transport-after-40/) covers the wheel and deck choices that make rough sidewalks comfortable on older knees.

### Keep rest intervals purposeful
Chatting on the coping is half the joy of a morning session, so there is no need to skate with a stopwatch. Simply noticing when a two-minute breather turns into a fifteen-minute cool-down keeps your joints warm and your heart rate in that 5.0 to 6.0 MET training zone for the active portion of your hour.
