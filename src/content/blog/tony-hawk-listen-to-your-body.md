Tony Hawk, in a Reader's Digest interview this July:

> "You have to listen to your body. I am as guilty as anyone of not listening to my body and pushing through pain, to the point where I sabotage my own health. I don't do that anymore."

He's 58 and still skating. He's also had a run of serious injuries over the years, including a broken femur in 2022 that he's been open about the long recovery from. So the quote isn't him winding down. It's him describing a habit he dropped.

## What he actually gave up

"I don't do that anymore" is carrying the weight in that sentence. What Hawk stopped doing wasn't skating — it was overriding his body's signals to prove a point. Push through it, because stopping feels like losing.

Most adults coming back to skating in their 40s and 50s picked that habit up somewhere too, usually not from skating. Work, mostly. The idea that backing off is a form of failure is not a skateboarding idea, but it transfers easily.

## The outlier framing is a trap

It's tempting to file this under "Tony Hawk is a genetic freak, this has nothing to do with me." That's a comfortable read because it lets you off the hook.

The more useful read: one of the most physically gifted athletes the sport has produced still had to learn, in his fifties, to stop treating his body as a tool that ought to just comply. Nobody skates for forty years on willpower. Whether you're still skating in ten years has less to do with toughness than with whether you worked out how to stop when stopping was the right call.

## Applied to a normal Tuesday

Most adult skaters carry some quiet guilt about the things they've stopped attempting. Drop-ins above a certain height. Anything that needs a hard commitment to speed. Stairs, generally.

Some practical versions of what Hawk is describing:

- Ending a session early when something feels off, rather than skating it out
- Sticking to features you're confident on instead of matching the teenager next to you
- Treating "I didn't feel like pushing it today" as a complete sentence

None of this means skating less overall. It means skating in a way that's still available to you next year.

If you've been quietly deciding which parts of skating you're willing to let go of, that's not a retreat — it's the same calculation Hawk says took him until 58 to make.
