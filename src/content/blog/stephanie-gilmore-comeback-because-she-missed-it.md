Wanting to roll again rarely sounds like a serious enough reason to pull a skateboard out of storage. Adults often feel a return needs a weightier excuse—a health warning, a [disruption in a weekly routine](/blog/life-disruptions-best-time-to-return-to-skating/), or a structured fitness goal—rather than simply missing the feel of a board underfoot. Right behind that hesitation sits a second judgement a returning skater is often tempted to make: treating a clumsy first session or two as proof that the break was too long.

Eight-time world surfing champion Stephanie Gilmore's 2026 return to the World Surf League Championship Tour tests both assumptions against an actual season record. Unlike [Kelly Slater's return at 54 following hip surgery](/blog/kelly-slater-comeback-lessons-for-adult-skaters/), Gilmore was not marking the end of a surgical rehabilitation when she accepted a 2026 season wildcard at 38 after sitting out the 2024 and 2025 tours. Going into the year, she said she had missed competing and wanted to test herself against the tour's younger field again.

Her results across 2026 did not follow a tidy arc. She lost in the opening rounds at the first two Australian stops in April—to Luana Silva at Bells Beach and to Erin Brooks at Margaret River—before [beating Silva in the final of the Bonsoy Gold Coast Pro at Snapper Rocks in May](https://www.theguardian.com/sport/blog/2026/may/04/steph-gilmore-wins-world-surf-league-gold-coast-surfing-championship) for her seventh Gold Coast title and [34th career Championship Tour victory](https://www.foxsports.com.au/surfing/surfing-2026-incredible-scenes-as-steph-gilmore-wins-gold-coast-pro-for-seventh-time-on-wsl-tour-video-latest-news/news-story/23b598a6a1e3fa0c51b3bca63094929f), nine years after her previous win there. Four months later, on September 11 at the [Lexus Trestles Pro at Lower Trestles](https://www.surfnewsnetwork.com/lexus-trestles-pro-brings-world-title-race-back-to-southern-californias-high-performance-playground/), a [10.90 to 10.10 Round 1 loss to Brisa Hennessy](https://www.swellnet.com/news/form-guide/2026/09/13/2026-trestles-pro-day-1) left Gilmore outside the top-14 cut and ended her mathematical shot at a ninth world title.

Contest scores from the outside do not tell us why Snapper Rocks came together in May after two early losses in April, or how much the two-year hiatus mattered in a close heat in September. Winning on the Gold Coast did not prove that missing a sport erases time away, and falling short of the cut at Trestles did not make the April losses the whole truth of her return. Without a rescue narrative or a trophy condition attached, the practical choice for an older skater is small: put the board and pads in the car, roll around an empty patch of flat concrete for half an hour after work, and decide from there whether you want to go out again on the weekend.
