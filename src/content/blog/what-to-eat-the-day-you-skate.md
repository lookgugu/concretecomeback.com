For the first year of my comeback, I treated food as something that happened separately from skating — I'd skate, then eat whatever was around, and wonder why I felt wrecked the next day even on sessions that hadn't been especially hard. It took embarrassingly long to connect the two. What and when I ate around a session turned out to matter almost as much as the recovery habits I'd already sorted out around sleep and rest days.

This isn't a supplements post — [that ground is covered elsewhere on this site](/blog/joint-supplements-for-adult-skaters/) — and it isn't a diet plan. It's the practical, day-of and day-after eating that actually moves the needle for an adult body that's asking more of itself than it used to.

## Before you skate

**Don't skate on empty, and don't skate stuffed.** Both extremes work against you. Low blood sugar makes balance and reaction time worse right when you need them most, especially in a longer session. A heavy meal right before skating leaves you sluggish and uncomfortable moving around. The sweet spot for most people is a light meal with some carbohydrate and a bit of protein one to two hours out — something like toast with peanut butter, a banana with yogurt, or oatmeal — enough to have real fuel on board without feeling weighed down.

**If you're skating first thing, don't skip breakfast to save time.** A small carbohydrate snack fifteen to twenty minutes before an early session — even just a piece of fruit — is enough to top up glucose that's dropped overnight, without needing a full meal to digest first.

**Hydrate before you're thirsty.** By the time you feel thirsty, you're already mildly dehydrated, and dehydration shows up as fatigue and reduced focus before it shows up as obvious thirst. A glass of water in the hour before a session, and water on hand during it, especially in warm weather, matters more than most adult skaters give it credit for.

## Right after you skate

**Protein matters more than most casual adult athletes realize.** Muscle repair after a session that's put real load through your legs and core needs protein as the raw material, and a lot of adults chronically under-eat it relative to how active they actually are. You don't need a shake standing at the edge of the park — a meal with a real serving of protein (chicken, fish, eggs, tofu, beans, Greek yogurt) within a couple of hours of finishing is enough to give your body what it needs without turning recovery into a science project.

**Pair that protein with carbohydrate, not instead of it.** Carbs replenish the glycogen your muscles burned through, and doing that alongside protein — rather than a protein-only recovery meal — supports the repair process better than either alone. A simple post-session plate (grilled chicken, rice, vegetables; eggs and toast; a bean-and-grain bowl) covers both without any special timing gymnastics.

**Skip the alcohol on hard session days.** It's tempting to treat a good skate day as an excuse to celebrate, but alcohol interferes with the sleep quality your body needs most on the nights you're actually repairing tissue. Save it for a rest day if you're going to have it at all.

## The next morning

**A protein-forward breakfast helps more than people expect.** Overnight is when a lot of repair work happens, and starting the next day with protein — eggs, Greek yogurt with fruit, a protein smoothie — supports the tail end of that process rather than leaving your body running on whatever you had for dinner.

**Anti-inflammatory whole foods are worth leaning into, without overselling them.** Fatty fish, berries, leafy greens, and foods with turmeric are commonly cited for supporting the body's natural inflammatory response, and there's no downside to building more of them into your regular diet. Some people find tart cherry juice or a curcumin-rich meal helpful for next-day soreness; treat that as worth trying rather than a guaranteed fix; the evidence is more suggestive than settled, and consistent training and recovery habits do far more of the actual work.

**Don't confuse "sore" with "under-fed."** If you're waking up genuinely depleted — foggy, unusually hungry, flat — the day after sessions, that's often a sign your overall calorie or protein intake isn't matching your activity level, not just a normal soreness response. Adult skaters who are trying to eat "light" while also skating several times a week are frequently underfueling without realizing it.

## Keeping it simple

None of this requires tracking macros or overhauling how you eat. The version that actually sticks is small and boring: something with carbs and a bit of protein before you skate, a real meal with both after, protein again the next morning, and going easy on alcohol on the days your body is doing the most repair work. Get those few things consistently right and you'll likely notice less next-day soreness and steadier energy across a week of sessions — the same kind of unglamorous consistency that [makes recovery days actually work](/blog/recovery-days-for-adult-skaters/) in the first place.
