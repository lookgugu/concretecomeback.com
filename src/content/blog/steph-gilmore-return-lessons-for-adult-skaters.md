I came back to skating at 46 after twenty years away. That is a long gap to judge on two outings. You remember how a basic turn or a mellow bank felt in your twenties, your feet react late, and it is easy to treat two awkward outings as proof that the break was simply too long.

Eight-time world champion Stephanie Gilmore's [2026 World Surf League Championship Tour return](https://www.surfer.com/news/stephanie-gilmore-returning-2026-championship-tour) this spring followed a sequence worth keeping in mind when that doubt creeps in. Gilmore won her record eighth world title at Lower Trestles in September 2022, surfed the 2023 season, and then stepped away in January 2024 for a sabbatical that stretched across both 2024 and 2025. Accepting a season wildcard for 2026 meant paddling back out at 38 after two full seasons out of a contest jersey, with the open question of a ninth world title in the background.

Her return started with two early exits. At the opening stops of the 2026 Australian leg—Bells Beach and Margaret River—Gilmore lost in the opening rounds, going out in Round 1 to Luana Silva at Bells and to Erin Brooks at Margaret River. Heading into the third event of the year in Queensland, the most decorated surfer in the women's draw was sitting at the bottom of the tour standings.

The turnaround arrived in early May at Stop No. 3, the [Bonsoy Gold Coast Pro at Snapper Rocks](https://www.theguardian.com/sport/blog/2026/may/04/steph-gilmore-wins-world-surf-league-gold-coast-surfing-championship). Surfing her home point break, Gilmore worked through the draw and defeated Luana Silva in the final to claim her seventh Gold Coast Pro crown and her record-extending 34th career Championship Tour victory, moving from last place into seventh in the world rankings.

An elite surfer taking two years off is obviously in a different position from an older skater returning after twenty years away, and as we noted in [our Kelly Slater piece](/blog/kelly-slater-comeback-lessons-for-adult-skaters/), a pro's timeline is never a template for an amateur. Contest results from the outside do not tell us how long reacclimatisation should take or why Gilmore won at Snapper Rocks after losing at Bells and Margaret River; they show only that two early losses were not enough to write off her return. If your own first couple of sessions back feel wooden, you don't need a guarantee that the old rhythm will come back on schedule—you can simply pick a quieter corner of the park next week and give it another hour.
