When a city talks about Olympic skateboarding, the renderings almost always show deep competition bowls, broadcast towers, and teenagers flying high above the coping. None of that helps a forty-four-year-old who just wants a smooth flat bank and decent lighting on a Tuesday evening. I have [written before](/blog/how-the-olympics-changed-skatepark-culture/) about how skateboarding's Olympic debut in Tokyo softened some of the old gatekeeping around parks and made visible beginners more normal. With the 2028 Los Angeles Games approaching—and the [four Olympic men's and women's street and park skateboarding events scheduled for July 18–27, 2028](https://www.nbcolympics.com/news/skateboarding-2028-los-angeles-olympics)—the next two years matter for a more practical reason: they are the window when cities decide what local concrete, if any, the Games leave behind.

On paper, LA 2028 marks a structural step up for the sport. At Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024, skateboarding appeared only as a discretionary host-city addition. When the IOC moved skateboarding onto LA 2028's initial sport programme in February 2022, [World Skate hailed the decision as skateboarding joining the "permanent Olympic event schedule"](https://www.worldskate.org/skateboarding/news-skateboarding/3410-skateboarding-joins-permanent-olympic-event-schedule-from-la-2028.html). That "permanent" label still comes with an important caveat: the IOC approves the Olympic programme Games by Games, so inclusion on the core list for 2028 is a major boost rather than an unconditional guarantee for every future Olympiad. Even so, moving off the temporary guest list makes it easier for national governing bodies and municipal parks departments to talk about multi-year support—though Olympic status by itself never pours a neighbourhood skatepark.

Comparing Paris 2024 and Los Angeles shows how specific and bounded Olympic park projects are in practice. In Paris, while the Olympic contests ran at Place de la Concorde, building-materials group Saint-Gobain installed a temporary 400-square-metre, pinball-shaped public skatepark—the [Saint-Gobain Skate Game, built from eight 3D-printed concrete modules](https://www.sortiraparis.com/en/what-to-do-in-paris/sport-wellness/articles/316253-jeux-olympiques-saint-gobain-skate-game-le-skatepark-100-ecolo-demenage-a-puteaux) designed with the accessibility association Pratikable—on the Esplanade de La Défense from July 18 to August 11, 2024, before relocating it after the Games to the Île de Puteaux to join the town's existing park. In Los Angeles, the Olympic competition courses themselves will also be temporary: following [LA28's June 2024 Sepulveda Basin proposal](https://la28.org/en/newsroom/la28-updates-venue-plan.html) and the [updated Olympic venue plan approved by the IOC Executive Board in April 2025](https://la28.org/en/newsroom/la28-celebrates-updated-olympic-venue-plan.html), [LA28's skateboarding plan](https://la28.org/en/games-plan/olympics/skateboarding.html) places Street at Valley Complex 1 and Park at Valley Complex 2 in purpose-built temporary structures in the San Fernando Valley.

## Showing up before the concrete is poured

For older skaters in Southern California—or in any city where council members point to Olympic momentum when updating a parks master plan—the lesson from Paris and the Sepulveda Basin plan is straightforward: temporary contest venues do not automatically turn into [inclusive all-ages skateparks](/blog/inclusive-skateparks-all-ages/). When cities open public comment on new builds or refurbishments over the next two years, the people who participate help shape the brief.

In my view as a community organiser, if only advanced riders weigh in when a city plans a park, the brief naturally tilts toward steep transitions and tight run-ups that leave older beginners and parents watching from the car park. Showing up to a municipal recreation hearing—or backing a local skatepark advocacy group—to ask for wide flatground bays, mellow entry slopes, shade, and evening lights is unglamorous work. Yet it does the same thing on a civic level that a welcoming group session does on a Sunday morning: it makes sure the space is built so a cautious forty-five-year-old and a fast teenager can share the concrete without anyone getting crowded out.
