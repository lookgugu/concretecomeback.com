## Post 1 (255 chars)

Most skateboard wheel-bite charts assume a 140 lb rider on factory 90A cushions.

At 180 to 220+ lbs, you compress truck bushings deeper into every carve and hit wheel bite about 2mm earlier in wheel diameter when stepping up to 54mm or 56mm wheels.

## Post 2 (366 chars)

Every 1mm jump in wheel diameter moves the top edge of the urethane 0.5mm closer to the wood. Going from 52mm street wheels to 56mm park wheels shrinks your gap by 2mm (5/64").

A single 1/8" (3.2mm) pad restores that 2mm loss with 1.2mm to spare, provided a 180 to 220+ lb rider also swaps soft stock cushions for weight-matched 94A to 96A bushings.

## Post 3 (391 chars)

If you keep soft factory bushings on 56mm to 60mm wheels, you need a 1/4" (6.4mm) hard plastic riser to stop the wheel grabbing the ply. That extra height has a cost on longer sessions.

Lifting the deck a quarter-inch forces a deeper single-leg squat in your front quad and patellar tendon on every push, and it steepens the arc your tail has to travel before striking concrete.

## Post 4 (398 chars)

Hardware bolt math from the countersunk head to the thread tip:

- Bare deck: 7/8" or 1" bolts
- 1/8" shock pad or riser: 1" minimum (1 1/8" starts much easier on uncompressed new rubber)
- 1/4" hard riser: 1 1/4" bolts

Never stack a rubber pad on a plastic riser (two sliding interfaces let bolts flex and shear), and stop tightening rubber pads once the bolt heads sit flush with the grip.
