## Slide-by-Slide Text (for `/creative` production, 1080x1350 4:5 canvas)

### Slide 1 (Cover)
- **Headline:** Riser Pads vs. Shock Pads for 180-220+ lb Older Skaters
- **Subhead:** Why heavier carves hit wheel bite 2mm earlier in wheel size, and the exact pad and bolt math for 54mm to 60mm setups.
- **Visual note:** Split macro diagram of a truck baseplate over a 7-ply maple deck: left side showing a rigid 1/4" plastic riser with a 58mm wheel, right side showing a 1/8" rubber shock pad compressing 0.5mm under the locknut with a 54mm wheel.

### Slide 2 (The Mechanical Split)
- **Header:** Same Mounting Holes, Opposite Jobs
- **Hard Plastic Risers (`1/8"`, `1/4"`, `1/2"`):** Rigid polypropylene or nylon spacers. Zero compression under bolts. Built to hold a fixed gap between 56mm-60mm+ wheels and the deck during deep carves.
- **Soft Rubber Shock Pads (`1/8"` / `3.2mm`):** 80A-90A urethane or rubber cushions. Compress 0.5mm-1.0mm under locknuts. Built to break the metal-to-wood acoustic bridge, damp coarse asphalt buzz, and prevent bolt-hole pressure cracks.

### Slide 3 (The Impact Myth)
- **Header:** Vibration Attenuation vs. Drop Impact
- **What 3.2mm of rubber does:** Filters out high-frequency road chatter from weathered tarmac before it numbs your intrinsic arch muscles over a 20- to 30-minute session.
- **What 3.2mm of rubber cannot do:** Once four mounting bolts pre-compress the pad, 3.2mm of rubber cannot absorb the vertical ground-reaction force of a stiff-legged drop. Softer wheels, cupsole shoes, and knee flexion carry the landing load.

### Slide 4 (The 180-220+ lb Wheel-Bite Math)
- **Header:** Why Rider Weight Shifts the Chart by 2mm
- **The radius math:** Every 1mm increase in wheel diameter moves the top edge of the wheel 0.5mm closer to the deck (`52mm` to `56mm` = `2mm` less clearance).
- **The weight factor:** Standard shop charts assume a 140 lb rider on factory 90A cushions. At 180 to 220+ lbs, you compress bushings deeper on every carve.
- **The 56mm fix:** Pair a single `1/8"` (`3.2mm`) pad with weight-matched `94A` to `96A` bushings on mid/high trucks, or step up to a `1/4"` hard riser.

### Slide 5 (The Hidden Cost of Tall Risers)
- **Header:** Why 1/4" and 1/2" Risers Tire Your Front Leg
- **Pushing fatigue:** Every push is a partial single-leg squat on your front leg. Raising the deck 1/4" to 1/2" forces your front knee and patellar tendon through a deeper drop on every push across a 40-minute cruiser session.
- **Tail timing:** Taller risers steepen the tail angle, delaying the wood strike on flatground ollies and curb lifts. Keep the setup as low as your wheel clearance allows.

### Slide 6 (Wheel Size & Rider Weight Matrix)
- **Header:** Pad & Bolt Chart (Mid/High Trucks, 175-220+ lbs)
- **52mm - 53mm wheels:** No pad needed (or `1/8"` shock pad for rough asphalt) · `7/8"` or `1"` bolts (`1"` with pad)
- **54mm - 55mm wheels:** `1/8"` shock pad or riser (unless running `94A+` bushings) · `1"` or `1 1/8"` bolts
- **56mm - 57mm wheels:** `1/8"` hard riser + `94A+` bushings, or `1/4"` hard riser · `1 1/8"` (`1/8"` pad) or `1 1/4"` (`1/4"` pad)
- **58mm - 60mm+ wheels:** `1/4"` hard riser + milled wheel wells · `1 1/4"` bolts

### Slide 7 (Two Bench Rules When Mounting)
- **Header:** Two Shop-Bench Rules Before You Skate
- **1. Never stack two pads:** Putting a rubber shock pad on top of a hard plastic riser creates two sliding interfaces that let mounting bolts flex and shear under side-load.
- **2. Do not crush the rubber flat:** Tighten nylon locknuts until the bolt heads sit flush with the grip tape and the rubber barely bulges at the baseplate edge. Cranking `1/8"` rubber flat squeezes out its elastic give.

## Native Caption

At 190+ lbs, you hit wheel bite 2mm earlier in wheel size than standard shop charts say.

Most returning older skaters swap tiny street wheels for softer 54mm to 58mm urethane inside their first month back. Five minutes later at the counter comes the next question: plastic risers, rubber shock pads, or bare baseplates?

Every 1mm jump in wheel diameter moves the wheel edge 0.5mm closer to the maple ply. Going from 52mm to 56mm shrinks your clearance by 2mm (5/64"). A single 1/8" (3.2mm) pad restores that gap and filters out the high-frequency asphalt buzz that leaves your feet tingling after twenty minutes.

What 3.2mm of pre-compressed rubber cannot do is absorb heavy vertical drop landings or stop a 190-pound carve on soft factory 90A cushions. If you ride 56mm wheels at 180 to 220+ lbs, pair a 1/8" pad with 94A to 96A bushings and 1 1/8" bolts so you keep your deck low for pushing without risking a lockup.
