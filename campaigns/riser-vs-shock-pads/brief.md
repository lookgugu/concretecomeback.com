## Phase 2: Source Extraction

### Core Insight
Hard plastic risers (`1/8"` to `1/2"`) hold fixed vertical clearance so `56mm` to `60mm+` wheels never grab the maple ply during a heavy carve, whereas `1/8"` (`3.2mm`) rubber shock pads break the metal-to-wood vibration bridge to stop foot numbness on rough asphalt without changing vertical landing impact.

### Supporting Points (Standalone Atoms)
1. **Vibration attenuation vs. impact absorption:** A `1/8"` (`3.2mm`) rubber shock pad filters out high-frequency road buzz from coarse asphalt that fatigues the intrinsic muscles of the foot arch over a 20- to 30-minute session, and prevents bolt-hole pressure cracks. Once pre-compressed by four mounting bolts, `3.2mm` of rubber cannot absorb heavy vertical drop landings; wheels, cupsoles, and knee flexion carry that load.
2. **Rider weight shifts the wheel-bite threshold by ~2mm:** Standard wheel-bite charts assume a ~140 lb teenager on factory `90A` bushings. An older skater weighing `180` to `220+ lbs` compresses bushings deeper on a carve, hitting wheel bite roughly `2mm` earlier in wheel diameter unless paired with firmer `94A` to `96A` bushings.
3. **The radius math of stepping up wheel sizes:** Every `1mm` increase in wheel diameter only moves the top edge of the wheel `0.5mm` closer to the deck. Stepping from a `52mm` street wheel to a `56mm` park/hybrid wheel reduces clearance by `2mm` (`5/64"`), which a single `1/8"` (`3.2mm`) pad fully restores with `1.2mm` to spare.
4. **Tall risers tax the front quad and slow tail timing:** Stepping up to `1/4"` (`6.4mm`) or `1/2"` (`12.7mm`) risers raises the standing deck height enough to force a deeper single-leg squat in the front knee and patellar tendon on every push over a 40-minute session, while increasing the tail angle and delaying flatground pop.
5. **Exact hardware bolt length and torque rules:** Bare decks use `7/8"` or `1"` bolts; `1/8"` pads take `1"` or `1 1/8"` bolts (`1 1/8"` is easier to thread onto uncompressed new rubber); `1/4"` risers require `1 1/4"` bolts so at least one full thread clears the nylon lock insert. Stacking rubber on plastic creates two sliding interfaces that let bolts flex and shear, and cranking rubber pads flat ruins their elastic give.

### Key Data Points
- **Hard riser thicknesses:** `1/8"` (`3.2mm`), `1/4"` (`6.4mm`), `1/2"` (`12.7mm`), zero bolt compression.
- **Soft shock pad thicknesses:** `1/8"` (`3.2mm`), occasionally `1/16"` (`1.6mm`), `80A` to `90A` polyurethane or rubber, compresses `~0.5mm` to `1.0mm` under locknuts.
- **Clearance math:** `52mm` to `56mm` wheel jump = `4mm` diameter increase = `2mm` (`5/64"`) radius increase toward the deck.
- **Weight & bushing pairing:** Riders `175` to `220+ lbs` on `56mm` to `57mm` wheels with mid/high trucks need either a `1/8"` hard riser with `94A` to `96A` bushings or a `1/4"` riser.
- **Hardware bolt chart:** No pad = `7/8"` to `1"`; `1/8"` pad = `1"` minimum / `1 1/8"` easy-start; `1/4"` riser = `1 1/4"`; `1/2"` riser = `1 1/2"`.

### Contrarian Takes / Myth Corrections
- Marketing claims that `1/8"` rubber shock pads "save your knees on drops" conflate high-frequency road vibration with vertical ground-reaction force. Three millimeters of pre-compressed rubber stops your feet from buzzing on weathered tarmac; it does nothing for a stiff-legged drop.
- Stacking a rubber shock pad on top of a hard plastic riser to get both height and cushion introduces two sliding interfaces that allow mounting bolts to flex and shear under side-load.
- Rubbing skate wax on the deck only buys a fraction of a second on a light rub; it will not stop a lockup when a `56mm` or `60mm` wheel presses into the maple ply under a `190 lb` carve.
