## Verbatim Source Quotes & Evidence Anchors Used

1. **Center-to-rail drop & flat center belly comparison (Lines 97, 119-129, 174-175):**
   - "Mellow concave (`0.30\"` to `0.35\"` of center-to-rail drop) gives your sole a broader, flatter platform that reduces intrinsic foot muscle cramping during bowl sessions, transition pumping, and cruising. Steep concave (`0.48\"` to `0.55\"+` of rail drop) creates deep toe and heel pockets that catch your shoe for faster kickflips, while concentrating downward pressure along the outer edges of the foot."
   - Center-to-Rail Drop (on an `8.5"` deck): Mellow = `0.30"` to `0.35"` (`7.5mm` to `9.0mm`), Flat Center Belly Width = `3.5"` to `4.5"`; Medium = `0.38"` to `0.45"` (`9.5mm` to `11.5mm`), Flat Center Belly Width = `2.5"` to `3.25"`; Steep = `0.48"` to `0.55"+` (`12.0mm` to `14.0mm+`), Flat Center Belly Width = `1.5"` to `2.25"` tub or continuous radial curve.
2. **Cross-board vs. straight-forward pushing stance loads (Lines 103-116):**
   - "When your shoe sits across that trough during long pushes or transition carves, load concentrates on the first and fifth metatarsal heads and lateral foot border while the plantar arch bridges an unsupported gap, triggering rapid intrinsic muscle fatigue and arch cramping."
   - "After age 40, the plantar fascia and small stabilizing muscles inside the sole (such as the abductor hallucis and flexor digitorum brevis) fatigue faster when forced to hold tension across an uneven surface."
   - "When you turn your front foot forward to push down the street or across a park flat bottom, the outer rails of a steep deck press upward against the inside and outside edges of your shoe, squeezing the forefoot inward."
3. **Wide-deck concave multiplier (Lines 72, 130, 168-169):**
   - "Because concave curves upward continuously from the centerline to the outer rail, extending a press mold from `8.25\"` to `8.75\"` or `9.0\"` raises the outer rail height by roughly `0.05\"` to `0.08\"` even on the same radius mold. An `8.75\"` medium-concave bowl deck often has the same total edge rise under your toes and heels as an `8.125\"` steep street deck."
4. **3-step local skate shop carpet test & tail kick angles (Lines 134-151):**
   - "Walk into an independent shop like 35th North in Seattle, Uprise in Chicago, or Slam City Skates in London and tell the staff you want to compare a mellow mold against a steeper mold in your width."
   - "A deck with steep side rails almost always carries a steeper tail kick angle (`20` to `22` degrees versus `17` to `19` degrees on a mellow mold), which requires your back ankle to drop through a slightly larger arc before the tail strikes the concrete."
5. **5-row differential foot-cramping diagnostic (Lines 157-164):**
   - Row 1: Arch burns cross-board in a bowl, even in stiff shoes -> Steep deck concave (`0.48"+` drop) or deep wheel-well pockets -> Switch to `0.30"` to `0.38"` drop deck with wider flat center belly.
   - Row 2: Forefoot and arch ache on flat ground and rough pavement within 15 minutes -> Thin, flexible outsole and packed-out open-cell foam insole -> Cushioned cupsole skate shoes or `6mm` to `10mm` dense polyurethane sport insole.
   - Row 3: Cramping in toes and ball of foot during drop-ins or speed checks -> Reflexive toe-clawing -> Splay and flatten toes on flat bottom between runs; unlace bottom two eyelets by one notch.
   - Row 4: Whole sole tingles and feels wooden after 20 minutes on rough asphalt -> High-frequency road buzz -> Install `1/8"` soft rubber shock pads and step down to `87A` to `93A` wheels.
   - Row 5: Sharp, localized stab under the heel on first step out of bed the next morning -> Potential plantar fascia irritation or heel fat-pad bruising -> Pause high-impact sessions and review podiatrist guidance on foot care for older skaters over 40.
