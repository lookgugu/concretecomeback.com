## Slide-by-Slide Text (for `/creative` production, 1080x1350 4:5 canvas)

### Slide 1 (Cover)
- **Headline:** Mellow vs. Steep Deck Concave for Older Skaters
- **Subhead:** How 0.30" vs. 0.55" of rail drop affects your arches, why wider 8.75"-9.0" decks feel steeper, and the 5 causes of mid-session foot cramping.
- **Visual note:** Cross-section diagram of an 8.5" 7-ply maple deck with a metal straightedge resting across both outer rails: top profile showing a 0.30"-0.35" mellow drop with a 3.5"-4.5" flat center belly, bottom profile showing a 0.48"-0.55"+ steep radial trough under a shoe outsole.

### Slide 2 (The Straightedge Measurement)
- **Header:** Brand Labels Vary. Measure the Rail Drop.
- **How to measure:** Lay a rigid metal ruler across both outer rails midway between the front and rear truck holes over the grip tape. Measure straight down to the centerline.
- **Mellow (Low):** `0.30"` to `0.35"` (`7.5mm` to `9.0mm`) drop · `3.5"` to `4.5"` flat center belly · lowest cramping risk for 45-min+ sessions.
- **Medium (Standard):** `0.38"` to `0.45"` (`9.5mm` to `11.5mm`) drop · `2.5"` to `3.25"` flat center belly · pocket rise at outer `2"` of rail.
- **Steep (High / Full):** `0.48"` to `0.55"+` (`12.0mm` to `14.0mm+`) drop · `1.5"` to `2.25"` tub or continuous radial curve.

### Slide 3 (Why Steep Concave Fatigues Older Arches)
- **Header:** Two Mechanical Loads on a 40+ Foot
- **1. Cross-board stance (carving & pumping):** The ball and heel rest on elevated outer rails while the center drops away. Unless your shoe has a rigid midsole shank, your plantar fascia, abductor hallucis, and flexor digitorum brevis must bridge that hollow gap on every pump.
- **2. Straight-forward stance (pushing):** Steep outer rails press upward against the inside and outside edges of your shoe, squeezing the forefoot inward while your pushing leg cycles.

### Slide 4 (The Wide-Deck Multiplier)
- **Header:** Why an 8.75" or 9.0" Medium Deck Feels Steep
- **The geometry:** Concave curves upward continuously from the centerline to the outer edge.
- **The rail rise:** Extending a press mold from `8.25"` to `8.75"` or `9.0"` adds `0.05"` to `0.08"` of vertical rail height on the exact same radius mold.
- **The takeaway:** An `8.75"` medium-concave bowl deck often has the same total edge rise under your toes and heels as an `8.125"` steep street deck. Pair `8.75"`+ shapes with a mellow or low-medium mold.

### Slide 5 (5-Cause Foot Cramping Diagnostic: Part 1)
- **Header:** Is It the Wood, the Shoes, or Your Toes?
- **1. Arch burns cross-board in a bowl, even in stiff shoes:** Overly steep concave (`0.48"+` drop) or deep wheel-well pockets. Switch to a `0.30"` to `0.38"` mellow or low-medium deck with a wider flat belly.
- **2. Forefoot and arch ache on flat ground and rough pavement within 15 min:** Thin vulcanized outsole and packed-out open-cell foam sockliner. Move to cushioned cupsoles or add a `6mm` to `10mm` dense polyurethane sport insole.
- **3. Cramping starts in toes and ball of foot on drop-ins or speed checks:** Reflexive toe-clawing from nervousness. Splay and flatten toes on the flat bottom between runs; unlace the bottom two eyelets by one notch.

### Slide 6 (5-Cause Foot Cramping Diagnostic: Part 2)
- **Header:** Road Buzz vs. Morning Heel Pain
- **4. Whole sole tingles and feels wooden after 20 min on rough asphalt:** High-frequency road buzz traveling through metal trucks into the deck. Install `1/8"` soft rubber shock pads and step down to `87A` to `93A` wheels.
- **5. Sharp, localized stab under the heel on your first step out of bed the next morning:** Potential plantar fascia irritation or heel fat-pad bruising rather than simple muscle fatigue. Pause high-impact sessions and review podiatrist foot-care guidance for older skaters over 40.

### Slide 7 (The 3-Step Local Skate Shop Carpet Test)
- **Header:** 3 Checks on Shop Carpet Before You Grip It
- **1. Sight nose-to-tail at eye level:** Check how wide the flat center belly stays before the outer plies turn up (flat-bottomed tub vs. continuous radial curve).
- **2. Stand on bare wood in your skate shoes:** Rock toe-to-heel in your stance, then pivot your front foot forward to check for sharp rail ridges or an unsupported arch gap.
- **3. Check the tail kick angle:** Concave and kicks are pressed in the same hydraulic mold. Steep rails carry a `20` to `22` degree tail kick versus `17` to `19` degrees on a mellow mold.

## Native Caption

An 8.75" medium-concave deck puts the same rail rise under your arches as an 8.125" steep deck.

Because deck concave curves upward continuously from the centerline to the rail, extending a press mold from 8.25" to 8.75" or 9.0" adds 0.05" to 0.08" of outer rail height on the exact same radius mold. If your feet feel vise-gripped fifteen minutes into a bowl session on wider wood, the press mold under your grip tape is the first measurement to check.

On an 8.5" deck measured with a metal straightedge across the rails, mellow concave drops 0.30" to 0.35" (7.5mm to 9.0mm) with a broad 3.5" to 4.5" flat center belly. Steep concave drops 0.48" to 0.55"+ (12.0mm to 14.0mm+), concentrating load on your first and fifth metatarsal heads while your plantar arch bridges the gap.

Not every mid-session burn is the wood.
