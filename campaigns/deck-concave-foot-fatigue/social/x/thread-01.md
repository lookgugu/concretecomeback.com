## Post 1 (311 chars)

Lay a metal straightedge across both outer rails of an 8.5" deck midway between the trucks and measure the drop to the center wood.

Mellow concave drops 0.30" to 0.35" with a 3.5" to 4.5" flat belly. Steep concave drops 0.48" to 0.55"+, leaving a 1.5" to 2.25" trough that your plantar arch has to bridge.

## Post 2 (363 chars)

In a cross-board carve on a 0.48"+ steep mold, load concentrates on the 1st and 5th metatarsal heads while the abductor hallucis and flexor digitorum brevis hold tension across the hollow center.

Turn your front foot forward to push and those elevated outer rails press upward against both sides of your outsole, squeezing the forefoot inward on every stroke.

## Post 3 (445 chars)

Before replacing the wood, match the cramp pattern to the cause:

- Arch burns cross-board in stiff shoes: 0.48"+ steep mold (move to 0.30"-0.38" drop)
- Ache on flat + rough ground in 15 min: thin vulc shoe (move to cupsoles or 6-10mm PU insoles)
- Toe/ball cramp on drop-ins: toe-clawing (splay toes, unlace bottom 2 eyelets 1 notch)
- Sole tingles in 20 min on asphalt: road buzz (1/8" rubber shock pads + 87A-93A wheels)
- Morning heel stab: see a podiatrist

## Post 4 (398 chars)

Online catalog specs rarely list rail drop, and pressing an 8.75" or 9.0" deck in a medium mold adds 0.05" to 0.08" of rail rise over an 8.25".

At a local skate shop, lay bare decks on the carpet in your skate shoes:

1. Sight nose-to-tail to spot a flat tub vs continuous radial curve
2. Rock toe-to-heel and pivot into a pushing stance
3. Check tail kick (17-19 deg mellow vs 20-22 deg steep)
