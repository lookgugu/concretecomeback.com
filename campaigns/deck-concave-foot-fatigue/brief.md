## Phase 2: Source Extraction

### Core Insight
Mellow skateboard deck concave (`0.30"` to `0.35"` center-to-rail drop with a `3.5"` to `4.5"` flat center belly) gives older skaters a flat platform that prevents intrinsic foot arch cramping over 45-minute+ sessions, whereas steep concave (`0.48"` to `0.55"+` drop) elevates the outer rails for faster flip-trick leverage at the cost of concentrating load on the first and fifth metatarsal heads and forcing the plantar arch to bridge an unsupported gap.

### Supporting Points (Standalone Atoms)
1. **Deck width multiplies concave rail height:** Because concave curves upward continuously from the centerline to the outer rail, extending a press mold from `8.25"` to `8.75"` or `9.0"` raises the outer rail height by roughly `0.05"` to `0.08"` on the exact same radius mold. An `8.75"` medium-concave bowl deck often has the same total edge rise under your toes and heels as an `8.125"` steep street deck.
2. **Two stance-dependent mechanical loads on older feet:** In a cross-board stance (carving, pumping, rolling), the ball and heel rest on elevated outer rails while the center drops away underneath the midfoot, forcing the plantar fascia, abductor hallucis, and flexor digitorum brevis to act as a suspension bridge across the hollow pocket unless supported by a rigid midsole shank. In a straight-forward pushing stance, steep outer rails press upward against the inside and outside edges of the shoe, squeezing the forefoot inward.
3. **True straightedge concave measurement vs. unstandardized brand labels:** Woodshops do not share a standardized gauge for concave labels. Laying a rigid metal ruler or carpenter's square across both outer rails midway between the front and rear truck holes measures the true vertical drop on an `8.5"` deck: mellow is `0.30"` to `0.35"` (`7.5mm` to `9.0mm`, `3.5"` to `4.5"` flat center belly), medium is `0.38"` to `0.45"` (`9.5mm` to `11.5mm`, `2.5"` to `3.25"` flat belly), and steep is `0.48"` to `0.55"+` (`12.0mm` to `14.0mm+`, `1.5"` to `2.25"` tub or continuous radial curve).
4. **Five-part differential diagnosis for mid-session foot cramping:** (a) Arch burns cross-board in a bowl even in stiff shoes = overly steep concave (`0.48"+` drop) or deep wheel-well pockets -> switch to `0.30"` to `0.38"` mellow/low-medium deck; (b) Forefoot and arch ache on flat ground and rough pavement within 15 minutes = thin vulcanized shoe and packed-out open-cell foam insole -> switch to cushioned cupsoles or add a `6mm` to `10mm` dense polyurethane insole; (c) Cramping starts in toes and ball of foot during drop-ins or speed checks = reflexive toe-clawing -> consciously splay toes on flat bottom between runs and unlace bottom two eyelets by one notch; (d) Whole sole tingles and feels wooden after 20 minutes on rough asphalt = high-frequency road buzz -> install `1/8"` soft rubber shock pads and step down to `87A` to `93A` wheels; (e) Sharp stab under the heel on the first step out of bed the next morning = potential plantar fascia irritation or heel fat-pad bruising -> pause high-impact sessions and review podiatrist foot-care guidance.
5. **The 3-step local skate shop carpet test:** Online catalog pages rarely list true rail drop. Walking into an independent shop (such as 35th North in Seattle, Uprise in Chicago, or Slam City Skates in London) lets you run three checks before peeling grip tape: (1) sight down the nose-to-tail rail profile to distinguish a flat-bottomed tub from a continuous radial curve; (2) stand on the bare wood on shop carpet in your skate shoes and pivot from cross-board stance to straight pushing stance; and (3) check tail and nose kick angle at the same time (`20` to `22` degrees on steep molds vs `17` to `19` degrees on mellow molds).

### Key Data Points
- **Mellow (low) concave (on an `8.5"` deck):** `0.30"` to `0.35"` (`7.5mm` to `9.0mm`) center-to-rail drop; `3.5"` to `4.5"` flat center belly width; `17` to `19` degree tail kick angle; lowest cramping risk for 45-minute+ sessions.
- **Medium (standard) concave (on an `8.5"` deck):** `0.38"` to `0.45"` (`9.5mm` to `11.5mm`) center-to-rail drop; `2.5"` to `3.25"` flat center belly width;mild pocket rise at outer `2"` of rail.
- **Steep (high/full) concave (on an `8.5"` deck):** `0.48"` to `0.55"+` (`12.0mm` to `14.0mm+`) center-to-rail drop; `1.5"` to `2.25"` tub or continuous radial curve; `20` to `22` degree tail kick angle.
- **Width-to-drop multiplier:** Extending a press mold from `8.25"` to `8.75"` or `9.0"` adds `0.05"` to `0.08"` of outer rail rise on the same radius mold (an `8.75"` medium bowl deck equals the edge rise of an `8.125"` steep street deck).
- **Insole & shock-pad remedies:** `6mm` to `10mm` dense polyurethane sport insole inside a firmer cupsole shoe; `1/8"` soft rubber shock pads paired with `87A` to `93A` wheels for rough asphalt; 2 minutes rolling a lacrosse ball or cold water bottle under each arch before a session.

### Contrarian Takes / Myth Corrections
- Ordering an `8.75"` or `9.0"` deck advertised as "medium concave" online does not give you the same foot feel as an `8.0"` or `8.25"` medium deck; the extra half-inch to three-quarters of an inch of wood width rides further up the mold curve, adding `0.05"` to `0.08"` of rail height that curls under your arches.
- Not all mid-session arch burning comes from the maple press mold: toe-clawing during nervous drop-ins, packed-out open-cell sockliners in thin vulcanized shoes, and high-frequency road buzz through bare trucks each produce distinct symptom patterns that new wood alone will not fix.
- Concave depth and tail kick angle are pressed simultaneously in the same hydraulic mold, so chasing deep flip-trick rail pockets also locks you into a steeper `20` to `22` degree tail angle that forces your back ankle to drop through a larger arc before the tail strikes concrete.
